    Preparing Collagen Gels in Center Well Dish

    Version: 0.0

    Zhang & Brown Laboratories
    Author: Max Harkins (mharkin4@u.rochester.edu)
    Date: March 23, 2026

    Experimental Log:

    Please include a semi-detailed log of the trial.

    Key items include:

    1. Name
    2. Date
    3. Target volume of neutralized collagen solution
    4. Target concentration of neutralized collagen solution
    5. Measured pH
    6. Wall-clock duration of incubation
    7. Visible bubbles after incubation, if any

    I. Gather Supplies

    Basic items:

    • Center well dish (dish set)

      • Components:
      • center well: inner well of dish set; where the gel will be cast
      • dish bottom: bottom piece of dish set
      • dish cover: top piece of dish set

    • Styrofoam bucket for ice

    • Lab film (Parafilm M)
    • pH test strips
    • Eppendorf tube (2mL)

    Collagen Reagents:

    • 10X PBS
    • 1X NaOH
    • DI Water
    • Stock Collagen Solution (Corning® Collagen I (High Concentration, Rat Tail))

    If treating center well for adhesion:

    • Poly(ethyleneimine) (PEI)
    • Glutaraldehyde (GA)
    • Compressed Air Canister (Optional)

    If coverslip required:

    • Coverslip (18 mm Circle, No. 2)
    • Kim Wipes
    • 70% EtOH
    • RainX (Rain-X® Shower Door Water Repellent)

    Planning & Measuring Collagen Solution:

    IMPORTANT: If you are making anything other than 0.5 mL, 6% gel, follow official Corning® protocol.

    NOTE: For 0.5 mL, 6% gel ONLY, use below measurements: - 50 µL 10X PBS - 5 µL 1X NaOH - 151 µL DI Water - 292 µL Stock Collagen Solution

    II. Making Neutralized Collagen Solution:

    1. Fill Styrofoam bucket with ice

    2. Place all collagen reagents on ice for 20 minutes

    3. If center well treatment REQUIRED: Follow Treating Center Well Protocol below.

    4. Pipette 10X PBS into Eppendorf tube

    5. Pipette 1X NaOH into Eppendorf tube >NOTE: From my experience, it is best if you start lower than pre-determined volume and add a little more later on if pH is too low.

    6. Pipette DI Water into Eppendorf tube

    7. Using blue (1000µL) pipette, cut pipette tip ~3mm from the end

      NOTE: Collagen solution is thick and highly viscous - you'll need larger inlet area. 1. Pipette stock collagen solution into Eppendorf tube

    8. Scrape any stock collagen solution that remains on pipette tip back into the stock collagen solution container >NOTE: Collagen is expensive; this helps reduce transfer loss.

    9. With a free yellow pipette tip, mix the neutralized collagen solution thoroughly in the Eppendorf tube for 5 wall-clock minutes, trying not to make bubbles > NOTE: If bubbles are introduced, allow solution to sit on ice to dissipate before casting.

    10. Test pH on pH test strip: if pH is too low, add 1 µL 1X NaOH at a time until pH is in target range > NOTE: For ablation testing, aim for 6.5 ≤ pH < 8. For cell culture, aim for exact pH described in protocol.

    III. Casting and Polymerizing Gel

    1. Cut new pipette tip (similarly as mentioned earlier), and pipette desired volume of neutralized collagen solution into well dish > NOTE: If bubbles are introduced, pop them with a fresh hypodermic needle.

    2. Once satisfied with pH, label and date the dish cover, then place dish cover on well dish and wrap lab film tightly around the circumference of the dish set >NOTE: This is to make sure the dish set is airtight during polymerization in the incubator.

    3. Incubate wrapped dish set at 37.0˚C for 60 minutes > NOTE: Inspect the gel after incubation to ensure it is opaque; if transparent, incubate longer.

    4. Storage depends on whether a coverslip is required:

      • IF COVERSLIP REQUIRED: Follow Applying Coverslips Protocol below.
      • IF COVERSLIP NOT REQUIRED: To prevent 'gel' from drying out, pipette ~50 µL ?X PBS on top of the polymerized gel (use same osmolarity PBS as gel environment).

    5. Store wrapped dish set in the refrigerator, noting any bubbles if present.

    Supplemental Protocols:

    Treating Center Well:

    Treating the center well with PEI/GA may be helpful for improving gel adhesion to the edges of the center well.

    NOTE: PEI and GA evaporate rather quickly; replace green cap of conical tube promptly.

    Treatment:

    1. Pipette ~15 µL of PEI into the center well, making sure to spread the liquid so that it makes contact with the base and walls of the center well.
    2. Wait ~10 minutes to dry (you may use compressed air canister if impatient)
    3. Pipette ~15 µL of GA into the center well in the same manner.
    4. Wait until dry.

    Applying Coverslips:

    Adapted from Tresa Elias's Protocol

    If the coverslip is required (varies between experiments), you must place prepare it before placing it. It is important to coat coverslip with RainX to prevent unwanted adhesion of coverslip to gel.

    Coverslip Preparation

    1. Clean coverslips with EtOH and dry
    2. Place desired number of coverslips on a Kim Wipe
    3. Gently spray coverslips with RainX, then dab the surface with a Kim Wipe
    4. Repeat once more and let coverslips completely air dry

    Coverslip Placement

    1. Gently place coverslip on top of gel
    2. Replace dish cover and re-wrap dish set with lab film
